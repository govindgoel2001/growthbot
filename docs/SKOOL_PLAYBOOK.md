### 0b. "But shouldn't I launch *paid* first?" — the debate, resolved

The case for paid-first: launch paid so later free members see people already paying — proof the premium is worth it. **The principle is right** (paid members are far better proof than free ones; money is the only honest validation). **The execution — a $29 community launched paid-first to a standing start — is wrong**, for four reasons:

1. **It's the dominated option.** A $29 paid-first community is *too small to feel alive* **and** *too cheap to be high-touch*. It loses to free-first on volume/aliveness, and loses to a high-ticket cohort on commitment. There is no axis on which it wins.
2. **The proof is circular.** Social proof is an *output* of a working paid base, not an *input* you can front-load. Your first buyers would purchase an empty room with no track record — your coldest cohort buying your weakest product.
3. **You already have the validation.** 13 people pay for MyHermes at 93.75% retention; 2,000 emailed *asking* for products; a reel did 1,420 comments. Supply is validated. Paid-first re-solves a solved problem.
4. **It fights your brand.** Your winning hooks are "build it *free*" and "*stop paying*." You trained 8,500 people that you make expensive things free. A cold paywall-first move converts *worse*, not better.

**The resolution (freemium makes the binary false):** launch **free to join, with a founding paid tier live and visible on day 1**, in one community. Free joiners see paying members' badges *immediately* — the exact proof you wanted — without the cold-start trap. You bank money-validation on day 1 *and* keep the volume that stops it being dead.

Validate with money the low-risk way first; graduate to the high-ticket cohort once you have reps and testimonials. **Sequence the risk to your competence curve.**

## 1. The architecture decision: ONE freemium community, not two groups

This is the first fork in the road, and the 2026 platform changed the right answer.

### 1a. What Skool can do now (this is new — most playbooks online are stale)
Skool added **freemium + multi-tier** monetization in late 2025. A *single* community can now run: **Free** to join, plus **paid upgrade tiers** ($29, $99…) *inside the same group, same feed, same leaderboard.* Courses can be locked **by tier** (paid-only) **and/or by level** (engagement-only). (Appendix, sources 1–2.)

### 1b. Freemium (one group) vs the classic Nate-Herk two-group split

| | **Freemium — one community** ✅ (do this) | Two groups (free funnel + separate paid) |
|---|---|---|
| Feeling | One busy feed — free + paid energy combined | Two feeds, each emptier |
| "Dead community" risk at your size | **Low** — all activity concentrated | **High** — splits your few hundred members in two |
| Cost | **$99/mo (one Pro group)** | $198/mo (two Pro groups) |
| Solo-founder effort | One place to tend | Two places to seed, post, moderate |
| Upgrade friction | **In-place, one click** | Member must leave/join another group |
| Gamification | One shared leaderboard everyone competes on | Two separate, weaker leaderboards |
| When the split wins | — | Only once your free side is **10k+** and needs to scale as pure top-of-funnel (a year-2 problem) |

**Decision: launch as ONE freemium community.** `STRATEGY.md` invoked "the Nate Herk move" of a free funnel — that instinct is right, but Herk built two groups when freemium *didn't exist yet*. With freemium, you get his funnel **and** a concentrated, alive feed, for half the cost. Revisit the split only if your free base crosses ~10–15k (§11).

2. **You already own the free content.** The guides you've handed out over two months in docs *are* the free classroom. There is nothing to build — only to organize. The free community can be live in **days**, not weeks.
3. **A community is an asset you *own*; IG/email are rented.** Right now your 8.5k lives on Instagram's terms and your 2k in an inbox. Skool gives you a space you control, with notifications, gamification, and a feed — far higher engagement than email.
4. **The free community makes the YouTube course better, not the reverse.** Launch free now → you have a live, growing community to *point the course at* ("free community, link below"), screenshots of real members to use as proof, and a captive audience to test which builds people actually want before you script them.
5. **Perfectionism is the founder-killer.** Waiting for the "complete" course is the #1 way this never ships. Launch free, ugly-but-alive, this week.

## 5. Gamification — the anti-dead-community engine (your explicit ask)

"Alive and interactive and gamified like Skool is" isn't a vibe — it's a **mechanic**. Here's how Skool's points system actually works and how to weaponize it.

### 5a. The raw mechanic (exact, verified — Appendix sources 3)
- Members earn **points** when others **like** their posts/comments/replies. **1 like = 1 point.** (You can't buy points; you *earn* them by being helpful/active.)
- **9 levels**, exponential thresholds:

| Level | Points to reach | Feel |
|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 0 | join |
| 2 | 5 | ~one good first post |
| 3 | 20 | a few helpful comments |
| 4 | 65 | regular contributor |
| 5 | 155 | a known member |
| 6 | 515 | a pillar |
| 7 | 2,015 | months of grind |
| 8 | 8,015 | super-fan |
| 9 | 33,015 | legend (rare) |

- **Leaderboards**: 7-day, 30-day, all-time. **Custom level names** are supported. Crucially: **you can lock courses to unlock at a level.**

### 5b. The loop that kills "dead community"
> **To unlock the good stuff, you have to post helpful things → get likes → level up.** That single design turns lurkers into contributors. It's the entire game.

Design it like this — a **dual lock system**:

**LEVEL-LOCKED (free — drives engagement):** gate *free* templates behind levels so free members have a reason to participate, not just consume.

| Unlocks at | Reward | What it trains |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 2** (5 pts) | "Hermes Starter Pack" (your most-wanted template) | Make your **first post** → get 5 likes → instant payoff. The onboarding hook. |
| **Level 3** (20 pts) | Content-bot setup guide | Keep commenting/helping |
| **Level 4** (65 pts) | The morning-brief automation recipe | Become a regular |
| **Level 5** (155 pts) | The AI trading-bot written build (your 1,420-run topic) | The big free carrot — by now they're hooked and prime for Core |

**TIER-LOCKED (paid — the real builds):** the **video debriefs + full vault + weekly call** are locked to **Core ($29)** regardless of level. Points can't unlock these — only paying can. (Level grind earns *free* rewards; *building mastery* is paid.)

This combo means: **free members are gamified into being active** (which makes the community feel alive), **and** they keep bumping into the paid wall right when they're most motivated.

### 5c. Custom level names (theme them to your brand)
Default "Level 1–9" is forgettable. Rename to a builder's journey (Skool supports this):

`1 Lurker · 2 Tinkerer · 3 Builder · 4 Operator · 5 Automator · 6 Agent Architect · 7 Systems Engineer · 8 Automation OG · 9 Hermes Legend`

Members *screenshot and share* a cool level name — free marketing, and it makes progress feel identity-level, not cosmetic.

### 5d. Leaderboard prizes (turn the board into a reason to return daily)
- **Weekly (7-day) top 3:** a shout-out post + their build **featured** in the feed + a "🔥 This Week" badge. (Costs you nothing, drives everything.)
- **Monthly (30-day) #1:** a **free month of Core** (or a 1:1 with you) **and** their build featured **in a YouTube video** ("Community Build of the Month"). That last part is genius: it's a *prize for them* and *free content for you*, and it shows the whole audience that members get spotlighted → more sign-ups.
- **All-time top 10:** permanent "Founding Legends" recognition + a private room. Status is the cheapest, strongest retention lever there is.

## 6. Keeping it alive — rituals, cadence, and the "first 10 minutes"

Gamification is the engine; **rituals are the fuel**. Communities die from founder silence + no engagement loops + members who never got a quick win. Fix all three.

### 6a. The "first 10 minutes" onboarding (the single highest-leverage thing)
A member who gets a **win in their first session** stays; one who doesn't, leaves forever. On join, auto-route them through:
1. **Welcome post** (pinned) → "Do these 3 things now."
2. **Introduce yourself** thread → they post (and *immediately* earn their first likes → first points → a taste of the game). Make it a rule that everyone likes new intros — seed this behavior yourself daily.
3. **First win**: "Get your Hermes agent to text you in 10 minutes" → ties straight into the MyHermes activation fix (`STRATEGY.md` §9). The magic moment is their agent's first Telegram reply. *That's* the hook that makes the community feel real.

### 6b. The weekly ritual calendar (so the feed is never silent)
Predictable rituals train members to come back. Run these as recurring posts (you can template them):

| Day | Ritual | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | "**What are you building this week?**" thread | Sets intent, generates posts (= points for them) |
| **Tue** | "**Template Tuesday**" — drop a mini-template/tip | Recurring value, a reason to log in |
| **Wed** | "**Win Wednesday**" — post what you shipped | Social proof + dopamine + content for you |
| **Thu** | **Live group call / office hours** (Core) + AMA | The retention floor for paid |
| **Fri** | "**Free Repo Friday**" — the trending repo of the week | Feed from `topic_engine.py` — zero extra work |
| **Sat** | **Feature a member build** | Makes members the heroes → loyalty loop |
| **Sun** | **Leaderboard recap** + challenge/winner shout-out | Closes the gamification week |

### 6c. The founder rule
**You post and reply every day for the first 90 days. Non-negotiable.** A community mirrors its founder's energy. After 90 days, your top members (the Level 5–7 grinders you've been rewarding) carry it — *that's* what the leaderboard prizes are buying you: a layer of members who keep it alive when you're recording.

### 6d. Never open empty (the seeding rule)
Before the public launch, the community must look *lived-in*: classroom populated, **15–20 feed posts** already up (questions, a poll, a win, a template), and **~25 hand-picked founding members** (your warmest DMs/emails) invited 3–5 days early to start talking. Walking into a busy room makes people stay; walking into a ghost town makes them leave and never return. (Execution in §8a.)

- **Skool ↔ Hermes:** the community's quiet job is to fix the activation leak (`STRATEGY.md` §9). ## 12. Anti-fail checklist (why Skool communities die — and why yours won't)

- **Never launch empty.** Free *or* paid — seed it first (§6d, §8c). A ghost-town first impression is fatal.
- **Don't push a cold $29 wall first.** Founding paid goes to your *warm* list on day 1 (proof + early cash); the *volume* conversion comes via the cohort (wk ~2) + monthly challenges. Paid-first as a cold standalone tier is the dominated move (§0b, §7).
- **You show up daily for 90 days.** The community mirrors your energy until the leaderboard grinders can carry it (§6c).
- **Make the first reward trivially easy.** Level 2 = ~5 likes. The first win must come in the first session (§5b, §6a).
- **Don't over-price for ego.** $29 for volume now; $99 Pro for margin later. Volume = life (§3b).
- **Don't split into two groups yet.** One freemium community keeps the energy concentrated until you're 10k+ (§1).
- **Tie it to Hermes.** A community that gets members' agents *running* compounds with your product (§9). A community that just chats, doesn't.
- **The course is not a prerequisite.** Ship the free community this week with the guides you already have (§2a).
