## 1. The value ladder + the two-bank-account economics

People differ by **how much they want to do themselves.** Serve all three:

| Rung | For | The offer | Who gets paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DIY — Free** | learners, tinkerers | the cornerstone video + written guide, build it yourself | nobody (reach + trust) → feeds the two below |
| **DWY — $29 Community** | serious builders who want to customize + get help | templates + live build-alongs + advanced builds + the room + support | **you** (100%) |
| **DFY — MyHermes 1-click** | the majority who just want the *outcome* | click → deployed, hosted, running, zero building | **you** (50%) |

### The economics, honestly (at your 50/50 MyHermes split, MyHermes reinvested)

You're not choosing the "more profitable" one — they fill **different buckets**:

| | Community ($29) | MyHermes 1-click |
|---|---|---|
| Your share | 100% | 50% |
| Where it goes | **your pocket — income now** | **reinvested → your equity — wealth later** |
| Serves | builders (minority) | outcome-seekers (majority) |
| Role | your paycheck / runway | the asset + activation fix + company fuel |

- **Per head**, a $29 community sub (~$27 net to you) is worth ~2× a sub-$54 MyHermes sub. **Per total dollars**, the 1-click likely wins on *volume* (most people won't build) — but that money builds the **asset you own half of**, it isn't take-home.
- **The synergy:** the community paying your bills is exactly what *lets* MyHermes reinvest everything. Cover your personal income with the community → keep MyHermes lean → plow all product revenue into growth → your 50% equity compounds. One engine, two compounding outcomes.
- Illustrative, per **1,000 engaged free members**: community at 3% → ~30 × $27 ≈ **$810/mo to you (income)**; MyHermes at 10% (say $25) → ~100 × 50% ≈ **$1,250/mo into the company (your equity)**. *(Swap in the real MyHermes price for exact numbers.)*

## 2. Free vs paid — the split, and why giving away the videos is correct

**The principle:** your build videos are **not the product — they're the *ad* for the product.** A great free video makes 10,000 people think "I could do that." ~9,500 never will (no time, or they hit an error and quit). The serious ~500 pay to get it *done* — faster, with help, in good company. **Free sells the dream and proves you're legit; paid delivers the outcome.**

**The heuristic:** *Would it make a good YouTube video? → Free. Is it a file, a live session, or 1:1 help? → Paid.*

| FREE | PAID ($29 community) |
|---|---|
| The 5–6 cornerstone build videos (also on YouTube) | The **importable templates/configs** for every build (skip the 45-min follow-along) |
| Your existing written guides | **Weekly LIVE build-alongs + office hours** (fixes *your* error on *your* setup) |
| The AI-basics course (MCP vs API, agents, tokens) | The **newest builds, weekly** (you can't give away next month's — it doesn't exist yet) |
| The community feed + gamification | The **advanced/full versions** (risk mgmt, backtesting, 24/7 deploy) |
| The live challenges | The **builders' room** (network, leads, accountability) + **Hermes white-glove** |

**Why this isn't cannibalization:** every workout is free on YouTube, yet gyms/trainers/classes thrive — equipment, a coach, a schedule, people, someone to fix your form. The free videos *fill* the gym; they don't empty it. And the **better** your free content, the more the serious ones think *"if this is free, the paid must be unreal."* Generosity is the funnel.

**The same per-build example (trading bot):**
- **Free:** watch me build a basic bot — you see it's possible.
- **DFY (MyHermes):** don't want to build it? 1-click deploy, hosted 24/7. *(your product)*
- **DWY (community):** want your own strategy + risk rules + help? The Builders community. *(your $29)*

## 3. The platform — one freemium community, start on Hobby

- **One community, freemium** (free to join + a paid upgrade tier inside it). Not two groups — concentrates the activity so it never feels dead, and it's cheaper. (Skool added freemium/tiers in late 2025.)
- **Gamification, membership tiers, and level-locked courses are on BOTH Skool plans** — so the **$9 Hobby plan runs this entire playbook.**
- **The only meaningful difference is the transaction fee:**

| | Hobby ($9/mo) | Pro ($99/mo) |
|---|---|---|
| Fee per sale | **10% + 30¢** | **~2.9% + 30¢** (up to $899/mo; +1% above) |
| Admins | 1 (you) | unlimited |
| Custom domain / hide "suggested groups" | no | yes |
| **Zapier integration** (member sync) | **no** | **yes** (see §11) |

- **The rule:** build during the **14-day free trial** (full features) → settle on **Hobby ($9)** → upgrade to **Pro** only when member payments cross **~$1,267/mo** (≈45 at $29). Below that, Hobby is cheaper and loses you nothing. *(Going Pro also unlocks Zapier — §11.)*

**Net you keep per $29 sale:** ~$25.80 on Hobby, ~$27.86 on Pro. (Plus your MyHermes line, separate.)

## 5. The gamification engine — "evolve to make it relevant"

Skool's points are exact (verified): **1 like = 1 point**; **9 levels** at **0 / 5 / 20 / 65 / 155 / 515 / 2,015 / 8,015 / 33,015** pts; leaderboards (7/30/all-time); **custom level names**; and crucially **you can lock courses by level *and* by tier.**

**The dual-lock design (this is the anti-dead-community engine):**

**LEVEL-LOCK free templates** → free members must post helpful things → earn likes → level up → unlock. This is your "people need to evolve for it to stay relevant":

| Unlocks at | Free reward | Trains |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 2** (5 pts) | the most-wanted starter template | make your **first post** → instant payoff (the hook) |
| **Level 3** (20 pts) | content-bot setup | keep helping/commenting |
| **Level 4** (65 pts) | morning-brief automation | become a regular |
| **Level 5** (155 pts) | the trading-bot written build (your 1,420 topic) | by now they're hooked → prime for Core/MyHermes |

**TIER-LOCK the paid value** → templates-as-files, live calls, advanced builds are locked to **Core ($29)** regardless of level. Points earn *free* rewards; *paying* earns the builds.

**Custom level names** (theme to your brand): `1 Lurker · 2 Tinkerer · 3 Builder · 4 Operator · 5 Automator · 6 Agent Architect · 7 Systems Engineer · 8 Automation OG · 9 Hermes Legend`.

**Leaderboard prizes** (turn the board into a daily-return habit):
- **Weekly top 3:** shout-out + build featured in the feed + a badge (free).
- **Monthly #1:** a free month of Core (or a 1:1) **and** their build featured **in a YouTube video** ("Community Build of the Month" = prize for them + content for you).
- **All-time top 10:** permanent "Founding Legends" + a private room. Status is the cheapest retention lever there is.

## 6. Keeping it alive

- **First 10 minutes (highest leverage):** on join → pinned welcome ("do these 3 things") → "introduce yourself" (they post → earn first likes → taste the game) → **first win: get your Hermes agent texting you in 10 min** (or 1-click deploy a build). A member who gets a win in session one stays; one who doesn't is gone.
- **Weekly ritual calendar** (so the feed is never silent): **Mon** "what are you building?" · **Tue** Template Tuesday · **Wed** Win Wednesday · **Thu** live call/office hours (Core) · **Fri** Free Repo Friday (fed by `topic_engine.py`) · **Sat** feature a member build · **Sun** leaderboard recap.
- **Founder rule:** you post + reply **every day for 90 days.** The community mirrors your energy until the Level 5–7 grinders carry it (that's what the prizes buy you).
- **Never open empty:** seed 15–20 posts + invite ~25 warmest people 3–5 days before public launch. Walking into a busy room makes people stay.

## 9. The dual-CTA system

You earn on **both** paid rungs, so the only real loss is someone who picks neither. Rules:

- **Default CTA = 1-click MyHermes** — the masses, lowest friction, biggest total volume, and it *is* your activation fix (`STRATEGY.md` §9: the visit→running-instance leak).
- **Community = the deliberate income push to builders** — each one's worth ~2× a MyHermes sub to your *pocket*, so push it to the technical/semi-technical segment.
- **Segment the nudge:** clearly non-technical → 1-click. On-the-fence/semi-technical → community (more $ to you + they'll use MyHermes to host anyway).
- **Don't bundle** (stuffing 50%-owned value into a 100%-owned product gets messy with your cofounder). **Cross-sell** instead: community members get MyHermes credits; MyHermes power-users get a community invite.
- **Every free asset ends with both:** *"1-click deploy it on MyHermes — or build your own in the community."*

## 11. Automating Skool (API / SDK / MCP) — what's actually possible

**Verified June 2026. Bottom line: no official API; official automation is Zapier (Pro-only, triggers only); writes are possible only via unofficial reverse-engineered tools — including an open-source MCP server — with real risk.**

| Path | What it does | Risk | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zapier** (official, **Pro plan only**) | *Triggers:* new (paid) member, new post, course updates → send to email/Slack/CRM/webhook. **Outbound events only — can't post/manage Skool.** | ✅ Safe, supported | New paid member → add to email tool + tag; new member → welcome sequence; sync to PostHog. The safe backbone. |
| **Open-source Skool MCP server** (unofficial) | AI-agent control of **posts, members, courses, notifications** via reverse-engineered endpoints. Plugs into your Claude/MCP stack. | ⚠️ ToS-gray, can break on Skool updates, uses your session token | **Reads/analytics** + **human-in-the-loop drafting** (draft Free Repo Friday → you approve → post). |
| **SkoolAPI** (`docs.skoolapi.com`) / **Apify "Skool All-in-One"** (hosted) | Reverse-engineered REST for posts/members; works with n8n/Make/agents. | ⚠️⚠️ Same as above **+ you hand your token to a 3rd party** (security) | Quick experiments only. Prefer self-hosting over handing out your token. |
| **Browser automation** (Playwright/Puppeteer) | Drives the UI like a human. | ⚠️⚠️ Fragile, highest ban-look risk | Last resort. |

**Recommendation (fits your automation-heavy stack without risking the account your business runs on):**
1. **Member/onboarding sync → Zapier** (it's the safe, supported path; another reason to be on Pro once revenue justifies it).
2. **Engagement automation → self-host the open-source Skool MCP server** (don't hand your token to a hosted 3rd party). Use it for **reads/analytics** and **drafting** (e.g., `topic_engine.py` drafts Free Repo Friday → MCP posts a *draft* → you approve). 
3. **Be conservative with auto-writes on your main community.** Auto-posting can look botty and risks a ban — keep a human in the loop for anything that hits the public feed. Reads are low-risk; bulk writes are not.
4. **Re-check before relying on it:** unofficial endpoints break whenever Skool ships. Never make a launch-critical workflow depend on a reverse-engineered write path.

*(A `skool_bot` could live in this repo later — same pattern as `content-bot`: MCP read → Claude drafts → human approves → post. Roadmap it; don't gate the launch on it.)*
